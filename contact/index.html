Before you call

Four things that make the quote faster

None of these are required — call us with none of them and we will still come out. But if you have them to hand, we can usually give you a realistic ballpark before we ever get in the truck.

  • Rough dimensions. Length and width in feet is plenty. A standard two-car garage is around 400–500 square feet, a three-car around 650–750. Pacing it out is close enough for a ballpark.
  • The age of the slab, roughly. Even “it is the original garage from when the house was built in the sixties” tells us a great deal about what prep to expect.
  • A photo or two of the concrete. Text or email them. Cracking, spalling near the door, dark oil staining and any existing coating are the four things that move a price, and all four show up in a phone picture.
  • Whether anything has been put on it before. Paint, a sealer, a DIY kit, or a previous epoxy job that is now letting go. An existing coating changes the prep entirely, and it is far better to know now than on the morning.

Questions

Asked on most first calls

How long does epoxy take to cure?

Most floors are ready for light foot traffic about 12 to 24 hours after the final coat, furniture and light use at 24 to 72 hours, and vehicle traffic after roughly 5 to 7 days. Full chemical cure takes about a week.

Those windows move with temperature and humidity, so we give you the exact dates for your floor before we start. And we would much rather tell you to wait two extra days than watch tire marks press permanently into a coating that was still green.

How long does an epoxy floor last?

A properly prepped residential garage or basement floor commonly runs 10 to 20 years. Commercial and industrial floors under forklifts, steel wheels and constant traffic are usually in the 5 to 10 year range before they want a recoat — and a recoat is far cheaper than a new install, because the prep is already done.

The single biggest factor is not the epoxy. It is the surface prep underneath it. That is exactly why we grind every floor instead of acid-etching it and hoping for the best.

Can you put epoxy over an existing floor?

Over bare concrete — yes. That is the ideal surface, and we grind it first to open the pores so the coating can key into it.

Over an existing coating — it depends. If the old coating is sound and well bonded, we can abrade it and go over the top. If it is peeling, chipping, bubbling or flaking, it has to come off first — and we do that too.

Over tile, wood, vinyl or carpet — no. Epoxy needs concrete to bond to, so those have to be removed first. We will tell you which situation you are in at the estimate, before you have committed to anything.
