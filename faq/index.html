How long does epoxy take to cure?

Foot traffic in 12–24 hours, furniture in 24–72, vehicles after 5–7 days.

Temperature shifts those windows, so you get exact dates for your floor before we start.

How long does an epoxy floor last?

10–20 years in a home garage or basement. 5–10 under forklifts and steel wheels, then a recoat — which is cheap, because the prep is already done.

Prep decides this, not the epoxy. We grind every floor rather than acid-etch it.

Can you put epoxy over an existing floor?
  • Bare concrete — yes. The ideal surface. We grind it first so the coating keys in.
  • An existing coating — maybe. Sound and bonded, we abrade and go over it. Peeling or bubbling, it comes off first — we do that too.
  • Tile, wood, vinyl or carpet — no. Epoxy needs concrete. Those come out first.

We tell you which you have at the estimate, before you have committed to anything.

How much does epoxy flooring cost?

Priced by the square foot. Four things move it:

  • Size. Bigger floors cost less per foot.
  • Slab condition. Cracks, spalling and old coatings add repair time.
  • System. Solid color is cheapest, flake mid, metallic premium.
  • Extras. Non-slip, striping, cove base, heavier build.

Do I have to empty the garage first?

Yes — completely clear, so we can grind it. Anything mounted to the wall can stay.

Short on space? Plan on the driveway or a trailer for a couple of days. We confirm exactly how long at the estimate.

Can you install epoxy in the winter?

Usually. Epoxy cures by reaction, not drying, but most systems want the slab above about 55°F.

In an unheated January garage that means we bring heat or book a warmer stretch. Heated shops, warehouses and basements run year-round.

Is an epoxy floor slippery?

Dry, it grips like any smooth surface. Wet, it can get slick — which matters in a garage in winter.

Fix: we broadcast fine non-slip aggregate into the topcoat wherever you want it. Flake floors already carry some texture. Tell us where it gets wet.

What is the difference between epoxy and polyaspartic?

Two resins, and most good floors use both.

  • Epoxy builds thickness and bonds hard to prepared concrete. Ambers slightly in strong UV, cures slowly in cold.
  • Polyaspartic cures in hours, stays clear in UV, tolerates cold — but it is thin, pricey, and has a working window of minutes.

So: epoxy base for build and bond, polyaspartic or urethane clear on top. “One-day polyaspartic” trades film build for speed — a real trade-off, not a free upgrade.

How do I take care of it?
  • Sweep or dust mop; wet mop with warm water and a mild cleaner.
  • Skip citrus and vinegar cleaners, and soaps that leave a film.
  • Wipe up gas, brake fluid and battery acid rather than letting them sit.
  • Put a scrap of plywood under a floor jack or jack stand.

That is the whole list — we leave a care sheet behind with it written out.
