Questions people ask before they book

How long before I can park on it?

Light foot traffic about 12–24 hours after the final coat, furniture and shelving at 24–72 hours, and vehicles after roughly 5–7 days. Cure times shift with temperature and humidity, so we give you exact dates for your floor before we start. We would far rather tell you to wait two extra days than watch tire marks press permanently into a coating that was still green.

What is hot tire pickup, and will it happen to my floor?

It is the failure everyone has heard about: you pull in on a hot day, the tires soften slightly, and a week later the coating lifts off in dinner-plate sized sheets when you back out. It happens when the coating was never mechanically bonded to the concrete — which is exactly what you get from a big-box DIY kit or an installer who acid etched instead of grinding. We diamond grind every floor down to open concrete and install systems rated for hot-tire exposure, so no, it should not happen to yours.

How long does an epoxy floor actually last?

A properly prepped residential garage or basement floor commonly runs 10–20 years. Commercial and industrial floors under forklifts and constant traffic are usually in the 5–10 year range before they want a recoat, and a recoat is far cheaper than a new install because the prep is already done. The deciding factor is not the epoxy — it is the surface prep underneath it.

Why not just use a DIY kit from the hardware store?

Because the kit is not the hard part. A box kit gives you a thin water-based coating and an acid etch packet, and acid etching barely opens the concrete — the coating sits on top instead of keying in. Add that most kits go down at a fraction of the thickness of a professional system, and you get a floor that looks great for one season and starts lifting the next. If you have already got a failing DIY floor down, we strip it and start over; that is a normal call for us.

Can you install in the winter here in Illinois?

Often, yes. Epoxy cures by chemical reaction rather than by drying, but that reaction slows sharply in the cold and most systems want the slab above roughly 55°F, not just the air. In an unheated Champaign County garage in January that usually means we bring heat in or schedule you for a warmer stretch. Heated shops, warehouses and basements run year round without any of that.
